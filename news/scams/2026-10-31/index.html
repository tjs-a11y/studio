Saturday, October 31, 2026 · Scam Watch Brief

FTC Pulls 19 Fake FTC Sites as Imposter Scams Stay Top Fraud Complaint

The FTC reports imposter-scam crackdown results, warns health plan marketers during open enrollment, and sues Dollar General over register prices.

The FTC says imposter scams have been the top reported fraud category. It has removed 19 websites pretending to be the agency itself.

Imposter Scam Crackdown Results

  • The FTC took down 19 websites posing as the FTC, so official-looking pages can still be fake.
  • Enforcement under the Impersonation Rule has produced more than $80 million in consumer redress.
  • That rule took effect in 2024, giving regulators a sharper tool against people faking government or business identities.
  • Imposter scams lead reported fraud, according to the FTC, making them the threat most worth discussing with older relatives.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Highlights Efforts to Combat Imposter Scams and Protect Consumers

Open Enrollment Sales Warning

  • FTC staff sent warning letters to 25 companies that market or generate leads for healthcare plans.
  • The letters flag likely deceptive or unfair claims about plan terms during the current open enrollment season.
  • More letters are expected on a rolling basis, so be skeptical of plan pitches that sound unusually generous.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Staff Warn Marketers of Healthcare Plans and Lead Generators Not to Deceive Consumers about the Terms of the Advertised Plans

Shelf Price Versus Register Price

  • The FTC and 20 states sued Dollar General over allegedly charging more at checkout than shelf tags and ads showed.
  • Regulators say the practice ran for years and cost shoppers millions of dollars.
  • Checking receipts against posted prices is a simple habit that can catch overcharges.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC and State Partners Sue Dollar General for Charging Consumers More than Advertised for Many Products

Your Data and the Price You Pay

  • The FTC ordered nine airlines, ride-sharing and food-delivery firms to explain how personal data shapes prices and gig pay.
  • The inquiry could reveal whether consumers are quietly charged different amounts based on their information.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Launches Personalized Pricing and Compensation Inquiry
