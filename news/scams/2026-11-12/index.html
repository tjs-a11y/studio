Thursday, November 12, 2026 · Scam Watch Brief

Court Freezes Phantom Debt Collectors; FTC Hits Retail and Rental Price Tricks

A court froze a debt operation accused of posing as lawyers, and regulators pursue shelf-price, rent and health-data cases.

At the FTC's request, a federal court froze a debt collection operation accused of pressuring people to pay debts they often didn't owe. Fake legal threats remain a classic intimidation scam.

Phantom Debt Collectors Frozen

  • A federal court temporarily halted the operation and froze its assets after an FTC request.
  • Collectors allegedly posed as a law firm or claimed ties to attorneys, a tactic meant to scare people into paying.
  • They allegedly threatened to contact employers and family members, so treat such threats as a red flag.
  • Many targeted debts were frequently not owed, so verify any debt before paying a cent.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Action Results in Court Issuing Temporary Restraining Order Halting Phantom Debt Collection Operation

Health Data Shared With Advertisers?

  • Five states joined the FTC's lawsuit against telehealth provider Hims and Hers through an amended complaint.
  • The suit alleges sensitive health information went to third-party ad platforms despite privacy promises.
  • It also alleges users were misled about billing and cancellation, so check telehealth subscription terms closely.

Source: FTC press releases: Five States Join FTC Lawsuit Alleging Hims and Hers Engaged in Deceptive and Unlawful Privacy Practices

Register Price Higher Than Shelf Tag

  • Family Dollar will pay $63 million to settle claims from the FTC and 17 states.
  • Shoppers were allegedly charged more at checkout than prices on shelves, tags and ads.
  • Comparing your receipt to the shelf price is a simple way to catch overcharges.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC and 17 States Secure $63 Million Settlement with Family Dollar Stores in Price Transparency Win for Consumers

Hidden Costs in Rent and Car Ads

  • Progress Residential and owner Pretium Partners will pay $49.75 million over alleged deceptive rental pricing.
  • The FTC calls it the largest enforcement matter it has taken against a landlord.
  • Separately, Long Island dealer Stream Auto Outlet settled claims its advertised prices were far below what buyers paid.

Source: FTC press releases: Progress Residential and Owner Pretium Partners to Pay $49.75 Million to Settle FTC and States’ Charges Over Deceptive Rental Pricing Practices and Other Unlawful Conduct · FTC press releases: FTC Secures Settlement with Stream Auto Outlet, Advancing Price Transparency for Auto-Buying Consumers
