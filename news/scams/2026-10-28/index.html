Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · Scam Watch Brief

FTC Targets Fake Passport Help Sites and Shuts Down Debt Relief Scheme Aimed at Seniors

Regulators act on government impostors in search ads, a debt relief scheme hitting seniors and veterans, and alleged register overcharges at Dollar General.

Scammers posing as the government are a top regulator target this week. The FTC sued a passport services operation, and a court shut down a debt relief scheme.

Fake Passport Help in Search Ads

  • The FTC sued GovAssist LLC, Entry LLC and Scott Zuckerman over an alleged government impersonation scheme.
  • The operation allegedly targeted people seeking passport services by posing as, or claiming ties to, the federal government.
  • Paid search ads were allegedly the hook, so a top search result is not proof a site is official.
  • The complaint also cites misleading promises and fake reviews, so glowing ratings alone shouldn't earn your trust.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Sues Perpetrators of Government Impersonation Scheme Over Deceptive Ads, Misleading Promises and Fake Reviews

Debt Relief Scheme Banned for Good

  • A federal court permanently banned the operators of an alleged debt relief scheme, at the FTC's request.
  • Seniors and veterans were the alleged targets, which matters if an older parent gets unexpected debt relief calls.
  • The operators allegedly impersonated banks, credit card companies and government agencies to seem legitimate.
  • On October 14, 2026, the court entered a $106.8 million default judgment against the corporate defendants.

Source: FTC press releases: Court Permanently Shuts Down Illegal Debt Relief Operation Following FTC Allegation That Scheme’s Operators Impersonated Government and Business Entities

Dollar General Sued Over Register Prices

  • The FTC and 20 states sued Dollar General over alleged long-running deceptive pricing.
  • Shoppers were allegedly charged more at checkout than shelf tags, price labels and ads showed.
  • The alleged overcharges cost consumers millions of dollars, so comparing your receipt to posted prices is worth the extra moment.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC and State Partners Sue Dollar General for Charging Consumers More than Advertised for Many Products
