Friday, October 30, 2026 · Scam Watch Brief

FTC Removes 19 Fake FTC Websites as Imposter Scams Stay on Top

The FTC reports progress against impersonators, warns health plan marketers during open enrollment, and sues Dollar General over register prices.

The FTC says it has taken down 19 websites pretending to be the agency. Its enforcement has returned more than $80 million to consumers since its Impersonation Rule took effect in 2024.

Fake FTC Sites Taken Down

  • 19 websites posing as the FTC have been removed, so scammers keep borrowing the agency's name to look official.
  • More than $80 million in consumer refunds came from FTC impersonation cases since the 2024 rule took effect.
  • The FTC calls imposter scams the top reported fraud category, so treat surprise calls from 'officials' with suspicion.
  • Hang up and contact the agency yourself using a number you look up independently, never one the caller provides.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Highlights Efforts to Combat Imposter Scams and Protect Consumers

Health Plan Pitches Get Warnings

  • 25 companies selling or generating leads for health plans received FTC warning letters during open enrollment.
  • The letters flag claims about plan terms that may be deceptive, so double-check coverage details before signing up.
  • More letters will follow on a rolling basis, signaling the FTC is still watching this busy sales season.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Staff Warn Marketers of Healthcare Plans and Lead Generators Not to Deceive Consumers about the Terms of the Advertised Plans

Dollar General Sued Over Prices

  • The FTC and 20 states allege Dollar General charged more at checkout than shelf tags and ads showed.
  • Regulators say the overcharges cost shoppers millions of dollars, so comparing your receipt to posted prices is worth the effort.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC and State Partners Sue Dollar General for Charging Consumers More than Advertised for Many Products

Your Data May Set Your Price

  • Nine airlines, ride-share and food-delivery firms were ordered to explain how personal data shapes what customers pay.
  • The FTC inquiry could reveal whether two shoppers see different prices for the same service.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Launches Personalized Pricing and Compensation Inquiry
