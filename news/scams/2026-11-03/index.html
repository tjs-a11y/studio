Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · Scam Watch Brief

FTC takes down 19 sites posing as the agency, flags health plan marketers

Imposter scams remain a top fraud threat, and open enrollment brings FTC warnings to 25 health plan marketers and lead generators.

The FTC says it has removed 19 websites pretending to be the FTC itself. Its imposter crackdown has also produced more than $80 million in consumer redress since 2024.

Fake FTC sites taken down

  • 19 websites impersonating the FTC have been removed, a reminder that scammers copy even fraud-fighting agencies.
  • Enforcement under the FTC's Impersonation Rule has yielded more than $80 million in consumer redress since the rule took effect in 2024.
  • The FTC calls imposter scams the top reported fraud category, so treat any unexpected 'government' contact with suspicion.
  • Real agencies won't pressure you to pay quickly; hang up and look up the official contact yourself.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Highlights Efforts to Combat Imposter Scams and Protect Consumers

Open enrollment: watch health plan pitches

  • The FTC sent warning letters to 25 companies that market health plans or sell sales leads for them.
  • The letters flag likely deceptive or unfair claims about plan terms, a risk while families shop during open enrollment.
  • More letters are coming on a rolling basis, signaling the agency sees an ongoing problem.
  • Before signing up, confirm in writing what a plan actually covers, not just what a caller promises.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Staff Warn Marketers of Healthcare Plans and Lead Generators Not to Deceive Consumers about the Terms of the Advertised Plans

'Risk-free' cancellation that wasn't

  • Riverside Payments Inc. and affiliate Cascade Equipment Leasing LLC will pay $600,000 over FTC and Washington state allegations.
  • Officials say the firms falsely promised savings and made 'risk-free' cancellation nearly impossible for small businesses.
  • Separately, the FTC and several states sued Toast Inc., alleging hidden fees and deceptive subscription and cancellation practices.
  • If a parent runs a small shop, review processing contracts for cancellation terms and unexpected fees.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC, Washington Take Action Against Payment Processor for Deceiving Small Businesses About Costs and Cancellation Terms · FTC press releases: FTC and States Take Action Against Toast Inc. over Hidden and Unauthorized Fees, Deceptive Subscription and Cancellation Practices and Other Unlawful Conduct
