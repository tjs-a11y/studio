Monday, October 26, 2026 · Scam Watch Brief

FTC Targets Passport Impersonators and Shuts Down Debt Relief Scheme Aimed at Seniors

Regulators act on passport-service impersonators, a debt relief operation that targeted seniors and veterans, and alleged checkout overcharging.

The FTC says a passport-services operation used paid search ads to pose as, or appear tied to, the federal government. If you're renewing a passport, be wary of sponsored search results.

Passport Help Posing as the Government

  • The FTC sued GovAssist LLC, Entry LLC and Scott Zuckerman over an alleged passport-services impersonation scheme.
  • Paid search ads allegedly made the operation look official, so the top search result may not be the government.
  • The complaint also cites misleading promises and fake reviews, so glowing ratings alone don't prove a service is legitimate.
  • Before paying for passport help, confirm you're dealing with the actual federal agency, not a lookalike middleman.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Sues Perpetrators of Government Impersonation Scheme Over Deceptive Ads, Misleading Promises and Fake Reviews

Debt Relief Scheme Banned for Good

  • A federal court permanently banned operators of an alleged debt relief scheme after an FTC request.
  • The operation targeted seniors and veterans by impersonating banks, credit card companies and government agencies.
  • On October 14, 2026, the court entered a $106.8 million default judgment against the corporate defendants.
  • If a caller claims to be your bank or card issuer offering debt help, hang up and dial the number on your card.

Source: FTC press releases: Court Permanently Shuts Down Illegal Debt Relief Operation Following FTC Allegation That Scheme’s Operators Impersonated Government and Business Entities

Checkout Prices Higher Than the Shelf

  • The FTC and 20 states sued Dollar General, alleging register prices routinely exceeded shelf tags and ads.
  • Regulators say the practice ran for years and cost consumers millions of dollars.
  • Small overcharges add up, so compare your receipt to posted prices before leaving the store.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC and State Partners Sue Dollar General for Charging Consumers More than Advertised for Many Products
