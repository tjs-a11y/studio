FTC Removes 19 Fake FTC Websites as Imposter Scams Lead Fraud Reports

The FTC reports progress against impersonators, warns health plan marketers during open enrollment, and targets hidden business fees.

Imposter scams remain the top reported fraud category, and the FTC says it has removed 19 websites pretending to be the agency itself.

Fake FTC websites pulled offline

  • 19 websites posing as the FTC have been taken down, proving scammers will even impersonate fraud fighters.
  • More than $80 million in consumer redress has come from enforcement since the Impersonation Rule took effect in 2024.
  • Imposter scams lead all reported fraud, so treat unexpected contacts claiming government authority with suspicion.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Highlights Efforts to Combat Imposter Scams and Protect Consumers

Open enrollment: watch health plan pitches

  • 25 companies selling or generating leads for health plans received FTC warning letters during open enrollment.
  • The letters flag deceptive or unfair claims about plan terms that likely break laws the agency enforces.
  • More letters will follow on a rolling basis, so confirm coverage details before signing up.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC Staff Warn Marketers of Healthcare Plans and Lead Generators Not to Deceive Consumers about the Terms of the Advertised Plans

Small businesses hit by hidden fees

  • $600,000 settlement: Riverside Payments and an affiliate allegedly promised savings and blocked 'risk-free' cancellations.
  • The FTC and several states sued Toast Inc., alleging hidden fees and deceptive subscription and cancellation practices aimed at restaurants.
  • Small business owners should review processing contracts for cancellation terms and unexpected charges.

Source: FTC press releases: FTC, Washington Take Action Against Payment Processor for Deceiving Small Businesses About Costs and Cancellation Terms · FTC press releases: FTC and States Take Action Against Toast Inc. over Hidden and Unauthorized Fees, Deceptive Subscription and Cancellation Practices and Other Unlawful Conduct
