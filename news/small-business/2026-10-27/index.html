IRS Partially Withdraws Section 355 Proposal; Key Year-End Tax Rules to Check

The IRS pulled back part of its proposed spin-off rules, plus reminders on installment sales, fuel tax records and disaster losses.

IRS Scales Back Spin-Off Proposal

  • The IRS partially withdrew proposed Section 355 rules on the device prohibition and active trade or business test.
  • Those tests decide whether a corporation can distribute a controlled subsidiary's stock without recognizing income, gain or loss.
  • Corporations and their shareholders and security holders are affected, so owners planning a split should revisit their timelines.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Guidance Under Section 355 Concerning Device and Active Trade or Business; Partial Withdrawal

Big Installment Sales Carry Interest

  • Section 453A adds an annual interest charge on deferred tax once a seller's installment obligations for the year top $5 million.
  • Owners selling a business on payment terms should model this cost before closing, not after.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Structured Installment Sales Over $5 Million: What CPAs Should Model Before Closing

Truckers: Keep Four Years of Records

  • The IFTA Procedures Manual requires licensees to keep distance and fuel records behind each return for four years.
  • Missing mileage or fuel logs can leave trucking businesses exposed if a fuel tax audit arrives.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: The Fuel Tax Audit Your Trucking Client Is Not Ready For

Disaster Losses Can Move Back a Year

  • Losses suffered in a federal disaster area may be claimed on the return for the year before the event.
  • That option can speed up a refund, though not every casualty loss qualifies for relief.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: No Tax Relief for These Casualty Loss Victims
