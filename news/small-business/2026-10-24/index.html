IRS Final Rules Clear the Way to Take Card Payments Directly

New IRS regs on card payments, a preparer charged over 4,335 returns, Colorado's tax ballot fight and two corporate tax updates.

The IRS has finalized regulations letting it accept credit and debit card tax payments directly, without routing taxpayers through outside processors.

IRS Can Now Take Cards Directly

  • Final regulations published 2026-10-19 implement Taxpayer First Act changes on paying tax by commercially acceptable means.
  • The IRS may accept credit or debit card payments itself, rather than connecting payers to third-party processors.
  • The rules also lift limits on the IRS paying fees under contracts tied to card-based tax payments.
  • For owners who pay estimates by card, watch for changes in how card payments are handled.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

Preparer Charged Over 4,335 Returns

  • Yuridia Trujillo, 43, of Meriden, CT, was arrested and accused of filing 4,335 fraudulent returns through her tax business.
  • The charges are allegations; the case is a reminder to vet any preparer handling your business or personal filings.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Ex-Connecticut Tax Business Owner Accused of Filing 4,335 Fraudulent Returns

Colorado Tax Measure Stays on Ballot

  • A Denver District Court judge ruled Wednesday that a graduated income tax proposal can remain on Colorado's November ballot.
  • The measure would replace the state's flat income tax rate, affecting pass-through owners who pay tax at individual rates.
  • The legal challenge to the measure is not over.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Denver Judge Rules Graduated Income Tax Can Stay on Colorado Ballot

Corporate and Court Tax Watch

  • The IRS partially withdrew proposed Section 355 rules on the device test and active trade or business requirement.
  • Those rules govern tax-free distributions of controlled corporation stock, so corporations planning spinoffs should check with advisors.
  • The Tax Court disallowed deductions for historic structure conservation easements, another setback for easement write-offs.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Guidance Under Section 355 Concerning Device and Active Trade or Business; Partial Withdrawal · CPA Practice Advisor: Tax Court Tears Down Deductions for Historic Structure Easements
