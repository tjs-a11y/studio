IRS Finalizes Auto-Enrollment Rules; SBA Updates Surety Bond and SBIC Programs

Final SECURE 2.0 auto-enrollment regulations arrive, SBA revises two programs, and a reminder on heavy-truck Form 2290 timing.

The IRS has issued final regulations on SECURE 2.0's automatic enrollment requirement for certain retirement plans. Employers that sponsor these plans and their payroll teams should review the guidance.

Auto-enrollment rules are now final

  • The IRS published final regulations on the automatic enrollment requirements under Section 414A.
  • They implement the SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022 mandate for certain cash or deferred arrangements and salary reduction agreements.
  • Covered plans must be eligible automatic contribution arrangements that meet additional specified requirements.
  • Employers that maintain these plans, plus participants and beneficiaries, are affected, so review plan terms and payroll setup.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Automatic Enrollment Requirements Under Section 414A

SBA trims surety bond paperwork

  • A final SBA rule streamlines the Surety Bond Guarantee program regulations.
  • Participating surety companies face lighter document submission and retention duties.
  • The rule also fixes conflicting provisions and updates the outdated Preferred Surety admissions rules and Quarterly Contract Completion Report.
  • Contractors that depend on SBA-backed bonds may see a smoother process.

Source: Federal Register: SBA: Rule: Surety Bond Guarantee Program: Streamlining and Modernizing

More leverage for SBIC funds

  • An SBA interim final rule implements the Investing in All of America Act of 2025.
  • It raises the maximum leverage for SBICs issuing standard debentures, in ratios of up to two-to-one.
  • Bigger fund capacity could widen financing options for small companies seeking investment.

Source: Federal Register: SBA: Rule: Small Business Investment Company (SBIC) Leverage Availability

New heavy truck? Watch Form 2290

  • The heavy highway vehicle use tax is due on the last day of the month after the month of first use.
  • Year-end truck purchases can trigger this filing, and a missing return can hold up the license plate.
  • Bookkeepers should flag December vehicle buys so the return goes in on time.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Your Client Bought a Truck in December: When Form 2290 Holds Up the Plate
