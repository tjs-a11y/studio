Sunday, October 18, 2026 · Small Business Money Brief

IRS Can Take Card Payments Directly; Social Security Wage Cap Climbs for 2027

Final IRS rules on card payments, a higher 2027 payroll tax cap, a dyed diesel penalty pause and proposed premium tax credit changes.

Final IRS regulations let the agency accept tax payments by credit or debit card itself, cutting out the requirement to route payers through third-party processors.

IRS Takes Cards Directly

  • Final regulations let the IRS accept credit and debit card tax payments without connecting payers to outside processors.
  • The rules implement Taxpayer First Act changes, which could reshape how businesses pay estimated and other federal taxes.
  • Restrictions on the IRS paying fees under card-payment contracts are removed, so watch whether payer-side costs change.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

Payroll Tax Cap Rises in 2027

  • The Social Security payroll tax cap goes from $184,500 to $192,300 in 2027.
  • Higher-earning owners and staff will owe Social Security tax on more wages, so update payroll and estimated-tax planning.
  • Benefits also get a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment, according to the Social Security Administration.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Social Security’s Payroll Tax Cap is Going Up in 2027

Dyed Diesel Penalties Paused

  • The IRS suspended federal tax penalties for dyed diesel sold for or used on highways nationwide.
  • The relief runs for the rest of 2026, easing risk for trucking, farm and construction operators.
  • The suspension is temporary, so fuel buyers should track when standard penalty rules return.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: IRS Halts Penalties for Dyed Diesel Fuel Use for the Rest of 2026

Premium Tax Credit Rules Proposed

  • Proposed IRS regulations would revise eligibility for and computation of the premium tax credit.
  • Freelancers buying Exchange coverage for themselves or family could see changes in what they owe for excess advance payments.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Premium Tax Credit
