New Congress Reshapes Tax Outlook as IRS Adjusts Opportunity Zone Reporting Rules

Midterm results shift Congress and the tax agenda, the IRS corrects proposed opportunity zone reporting rules, and a scholarship credit hearing goes phone-only.

The 2026 midterms flipped control of Congress, making a sweeping tax bill less likely and narrow bipartisan fixes more plausible.

Midterms Redraw the Tax Map

  • Congressional control changed hands in the 2026 midterms, resetting the federal tax policy agenda.
  • Large, sweeping tax packages now face longer odds, so avoid building plans around major overhauls.
  • Targeted bipartisan measures may gain room, which could still bring narrower changes affecting small firms.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: 2026 Midterm Elections Shift Control of Congress, Reshaping Tax Policy Outlook

IRS Fixes Opportunity Zone Reporting Draft

  • The IRS issued corrections to proposed rules REG-116506-25, first published September 11, 2026.
  • The rules would require qualified opportunity funds to file information returns and send statements to investors who dispose of interests.
  • Qualified opportunity zone businesses would also owe statements to funds holding their interests, adding new reporting work for affected owners.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Information Reporting Regarding Qualified Opportunity Zones and Updated Qualified Opportunity Fund Certification and Decertification Procedures; Correction

Scholarship Credit Hearing Goes Phone-Only

  • The December 15, 2026, 10 a.m. ET hearing on REG-117199-25 will now be held by telephone only.
  • The proposal covers a non-refundable federal credit for contributions to scholarship granting organizations funding K-12 education.
  • Clients weighing these donations should watch for final guidance before counting on the credit.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Federal Scholarship Tax Credit; Hearing

IRS Seeks Input on Withholding Paperwork

  • The IRS is taking public comments on the burden of its QI, WP and WT application and account system.
  • The request falls under the Paperwork Reduction Act of 1995 and mainly concerns foreign intermediaries handling withholding.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Notice: [Proposed Extension of Information Collection Request Submitted for Public Comment; Comment Request on Burden Related to the Qualified Intermediary (QI), Withholding Foreign Partnership (WP), and Withholding Foreign Trust (WT) Application and Account Management System
