IRS Finalizes Rules Letting It Take Card Payments for Taxes Directly

New final rules let the IRS accept credit and debit cards without a middleman, plus a data center tax push and a corporate spin-off rule change.

Final IRS regulations allow the agency to accept credit and debit card tax payments directly, with no third-party payment processor required.

IRS can now take cards directly

  • Final regulations let the IRS accept credit and debit card tax payments itself, without sending taxpayers to outside processors.
  • The rules implement Taxpayer First Act changes, so card payment options for business taxes may shift.
  • Restrictions on the IRS paying fees under card-payment contracts are removed, which could change who absorbs processing costs.
  • Owners who pay estimated or business taxes by card should watch for updated IRS payment options.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

Wyden targets data centers with taxes

  • Sen. Ron Wyden, top Democrat on Senate Finance, proposed an excise tax on data centers.
  • The same proposal would exclude data centers from certain tax breaks.
  • A companion bill would provide aid to displaced workers.
  • These are proposals only; nothing changes for businesses unless Congress acts.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Sen. Ron Wyden Pushes Bills to Tax Data Centers, Aid Displaced Workers

IRS pulls back part of spin-off rules

  • The IRS partially withdrew proposed Section 355 regulations on tax-free distributions of controlled corporation stock.
  • The withdrawn portion covers the device prohibition and the active trade or business requirement.
  • Closely held corporations weighing a split-up and their shareholders should check how the change affects planning.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Guidance Under Section 355 Concerning Device and Active Trade or Business; Partial Withdrawal
