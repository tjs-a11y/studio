Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · Small Business Money Brief

IRS Tax Pro Account Now Handles Business Client Payments

Tax pros can now pay for business clients online, the IRS fixes its opportunity zone reporting proposal, and California voters reject a wealth tax.

Tax professionals can now make certain payments for their business clients through the IRS Tax Pro Account. The IRS announced the change on Nov. 6.

Tax Pro Account Adds Business Payments

  • Tax Pro Account now supports certain payments made on behalf of business clients.
  • Bookkeepers and preparers may be able to handle some client payments without separate client logins.
  • The IRS announced the expansion on Nov. 6.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: IRS Expands Tax Pro Account to Allow Business Client Payments

Opportunity Zone Reporting Rules Corrected

  • The IRS published corrections to proposed rule REG-116506-25, first released on September 11, 2026.
  • The proposal would require qualified opportunity funds to file information returns and give statements to investors who dispose of investments.
  • Qualified opportunity zone businesses would also have to give statements to the funds that hold interests in them.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Information Reporting Regarding Qualified Opportunity Zones and Updated Qualified Opportunity Fund Certification and Decertification Procedures; Correction

California Voters Reject Billionaire Tax

  • About 52.9% of voters rejected the one-time, 5% wealth tax in preliminary results Monday evening.
  • Support came in at 47.1%, so the measure appears headed for defeat.
  • Business owners watching California tax policy can expect no new one-time wealth levy for now.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Billionaire Tax Rejected by California Voters

Trump Account Investment Rules Advance

  • The IRS issued a hearing notice on its proposed rules for eligible Trump account investments.
  • Those proposed rules were first published on August 21, 2026.
  • The rules limit which assets the accounts can hold before the calendar year the beneficiary turns 18.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Guidance on Eligible Investments for Trump Accounts; Hearing
