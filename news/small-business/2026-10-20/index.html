IRS Finalizes Rules to Accept Card Tax Payments Without Third-Party Processors

A final IRS rule clears the way for direct card payments, while proposed premium tax credit changes could hit self-insured freelancers.

The IRS has finalized rules letting it accept credit and debit card tax payments directly, cutting out third-party processors.

IRS cuts out card payment middlemen

  • Final regulations let the IRS take credit and debit card tax payments itself, no outside processor required.
  • The change implements Taxpayer First Act provisions, which could simplify how owners pay estimated and business taxes.
  • The rules also lift limits on the IRS paying fees under card-payment contracts, which could shape future processing costs.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

Premium tax credit rules may change

  • Proposed regulations would revise who qualifies for the premium tax credit and how it is calculated.
  • Freelancers buying Exchange health coverage should watch closely, since repayment of excess advance credits is also covered.
  • These are proposals, not final rules, so current treatment still applies for now.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Premium Tax Credit

Adoption credit refunds now flowing

  • The IRS has begun refunding more than 50,000 taxpayers tied to the adoption tax credit carryforward.
  • The agency expects to wrap up the refunds by the end of the year, useful context for clients asking about status.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: IRS Begins Issuing Adoption Tax Credit Refunds to Eligible Taxpayers

Scholarship credit opt-ins still pending

  • Sixteen governors, including Illinois' JB Pritzker, have not decided whether to join the Federal Scholarship Tax Credit program.
  • State participation determines whether taxpayers there can use the new credit, so client planning remains uncertain.
  • A researcher has projected how much scholarship money could reach public school students under the program.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Democratic Holdouts Still Aren’t Committing to Federal Scholarship Tax Credit Program · CPA Practice Advisor: How Much Will Federal Tax Credit Scholarship Program Benefit Public Schools?
