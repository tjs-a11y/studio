IRS finalizes rules to accept card tax payments without third-party processors

Final IRS card-payment rules, proposed premium tax credit changes, a red diesel recordkeeping waiver, and new state and city tax moves.

Final IRS regulations let the agency accept credit and debit card tax payments itself, without routing payers through outside processors.

IRS Takes Cards Directly

  • Final regulations let the IRS process credit and debit card tax payments itself, cutting out third-party payment processors.
  • The rules carry out changes from the Taxpayer First Act, which could simplify how owners pay estimated and balance-due taxes.
  • They also lift limits on the IRS paying fees under card-payment contracts, a change that could shape what card payers face.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

Premium Tax Credit Rules Revisited

  • Proposed IRS regulations would change who qualifies for the premium tax credit and how it is calculated.
  • They would also revise how taxpayers owe back excess advance credit payments, a key issue at filing time.
  • Freelancers and owners buying family coverage through a Health Insurance Exchange are the ones affected.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Premium Tax Credit

Red Diesel Paperwork Eased

  • The administration is waiving recordkeeping requirements tied to tax-exempt red diesel.
  • The aim is to get farmers, truck-stop operators and other businesses buying more of the untaxed fuel.
  • Fewer records could lower compliance work for these businesses.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: U.S. Waives Red Diesel Tax Record Requirements to Entice Buyers

State and City Tax Moves

  • Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson plans to again propose an employee head tax on big corporations, exempting less wealthy areas.
  • New York's volunteer firefighter and ambulance worker credit rises from $200 to $800 for individuals starting tax year 2027.
  • Married couples in New York will see that credit go from $400 to $1,600, worth flagging for clients who volunteer.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Chicago Mayor Proposing Corporate Head Tax Again, Sources Say · CPA Practice Advisor: New York Quadruples Tax Credits for Volunteer Firefighters and Ambulance Workers
