Sunday, October 25, 2026 · Small Business Money Brief

IRS Finalizes Card-Payment Rules Under the Taxpayer First Act

Final IRS rules on card tax payments, MFA expectations for firms, a Tax Court easement ruling, and a partial withdrawal of spinoff rules.

The IRS has issued final regulations that reflect a law change allowing it to take credit and debit card tax payments directly, without routing taxpayers to outside processors.

IRS Card Payments Get Final Rules

  • Final IRS regulations on paying tax by commercially acceptable means were published October 19.
  • They reflect Taxpayer First Act changes allowing the IRS to accept credit or debit card payments directly.
  • That could cut out third-party payment processors when you pay federal tax by card.
  • The rules also lift restrictions on the IRS paying fees under card-payment contracts.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

MFA Is Expected, Not Optional

  • The FTC Safeguards Rule and the IRS expect multifactor authentication on every system holding client data.
  • Tax and accounting firms that disable MFA over user fatigue risk falling short of that expectation.
  • Attackers are designing schemes specifically to get around MFA prompts, so staff training matters too.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: MFA Fatigue is Real. Turning It Off is Not an Option for Tax and Accounting Firms

Tax Court Rejects Easement Deductions

  • The Tax Court disallowed deductions tied to historic structure conservation easements.
  • Owners weighing easement write-offs on historic buildings should expect close scrutiny of their claims.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Tax Court Tears Down Deductions for Historic Structure Easements

Spinoff Rule Proposal Partly Pulled

  • The IRS partially withdrew proposed Section 355 regulations on the device prohibition and active trade or business test.
  • Those rules govern tax-free distributions of a controlled corporation's stock.
  • Closely held corporations planning a split-up should check which proposed provisions remain in play.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Guidance Under Section 355 Concerning Device and Active Trade or Business; Partial Withdrawal
