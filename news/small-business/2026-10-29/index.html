State Tax Measures Head to Voters in Colorado, Florida and California

Colorado weighs several overlapping tax measures, Florida's property tax amendment looks close, and the IRS Advisory Council schedules a public meeting.

Tax questions are on several state ballots, and the results could change what owners pay locally. Colorado alone has four tax-related measures that interact with one another.

Colorado's Stacked Tax Ballot

  • Amendment 87 and propositions NN, 136 and 137 all address taxes or tax refunds, each in a different way.
  • How they combine depends on which pass, so owners should watch the full set rather than any single measure.
  • The income tax proposal has faced bipartisan criticism and survived a legal challenge, signaling a contested vote.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: How the Tax Measures on Colorado’s Ballot Would Interact if Voters Pass All of Them—or Just Some · CPA Practice Advisor: Court Battles, Big Money and False Claims Swirl Around Colorado’s Income Tax Ballot Proposal

Florida Property Tax Vote Looks Tight

  • The amendment needs 60% support to pass, a high bar for any ballot measure.
  • Only one poll so far has shown it reaching that level, and polling overall is limited.
  • Property owners and commercial tenants should plan for either outcome until results are in.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Polls Point to a Close Call on Florida Property Tax Amendment

California Billionaire Tax Goes Social

  • California voters are deciding whether to impose a special tax on billionaires.
  • Paid social media influencers are increasingly involved in campaigning on the measure, so expect heavy online messaging.
  • The tax targets billionaires, not typical small businesses, but it shapes the state's broader tax debate.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: California’s Billionaire Tax Battle is Playing Out on Instagram—with Paid Influencers

IRS Advisory Council to Meet Publicly

  • The Internal Revenue Service Advisory Council has announced a public meeting in the Federal Register.
  • These sessions can preview IRS priorities, so bookkeepers and tax pros may want to track the agenda.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Notice: Internal Revenue Service Advisory Council; Public Meeting
