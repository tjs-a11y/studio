IRS Finalizes Rules to Accept Card Tax Payments Directly

Final IRS card-payment rules, a new charitable break for standard-deduction filers, and proposed premium tax credit changes.

The IRS issued final regulations letting it accept credit and debit card tax payments directly, without routing taxpayers through third-party processors.

IRS cuts out card middlemen

  • Final regulations let the IRS take credit and debit card tax payments itself, without third-party payment processors.
  • The change implements Taxpayer First Act provisions, which could simplify how owners pay estimated and business taxes by card.
  • The rules also lift limits on the IRS paying fees under contracts tied to card tax payments.
  • The final rules do not say what card fees taxpayers will pay, so check before charging a large tax bill.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

Charity break for non-itemizers

  • Beginning with tax year 2026, standard-deduction filers can also deduct qualifying cash gifts to charity.
  • Owners and freelancers who don't itemize should keep receipts for 2026 cash donations to claim the new deduction.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Donating to Charity? Your Generosity Could Pay Off at Tax Time This Year

Premium tax credit rules may shift

  • Proposed IRS regulations would revise who qualifies for the premium tax credit and how it is calculated.
  • They would also change how taxpayers repay excess advance credit payments, a key issue for self-employed Exchange enrollees.
  • Anyone buying individual coverage through a Health Insurance Exchange for themselves or family could be affected.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Premium Tax Credit

Forgiven student debt taxable again

  • A tax exemption for forgiven student loans has expired, a study warns.
  • Borrowers, including owners and employees, could face an unexpected IRS bill on forgiven balances.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Student Loan Forgiveness is a Ticking Tax Bomb Again, Study Warns
