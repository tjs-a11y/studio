IRS Corrects Opportunity Zone Reporting Proposal as SBA Streamlines Surety Bond Rules

The IRS corrected its draft opportunity zone reporting rules, the SBA eased surety bond paperwork, and a tax credit hearing moves to phone-only.

The IRS issued corrections to its proposed rules requiring opportunity zone funds and businesses to file new information returns and investor statements.

Opportunity Zone Reporting Draft Corrected

  • The IRS corrected proposed regulations REG-116506-25, first published September 11, 2026; check that you are reviewing the fixed text.
  • Qualified opportunity funds would file information returns and send statements to investors who dispose of their stakes.
  • Qualified opportunity zone businesses would furnish statements to the funds holding interests in them, adding a new reporting duty.
  • The proposal also updates how funds are certified and decertified, which matters for clients who plan to keep fund status.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Information Reporting Regarding Qualified Opportunity Zones and Updated Qualified Opportunity Fund Certification and Decertification Procedures; Correction

SBA Eases Surety Bond Paperwork

  • The SBA finalized changes to its Surety Bond Guarantee program, reducing what participating sureties must submit and retain.
  • This matters to small contractors that depend on SBA-backed bonds to bid for work.
  • The rule also fixes conflicting provisions and revises outdated Preferred Surety admissions requirements.
  • The Quarterly Contract Completion Report is also being revised under the final rule.

Source: Federal Register: SBA: Rule: Surety Bond Guarantee Program: Streamlining and Modernizing

Scholarship Credit Hearing Goes Phone-Only

  • The December 15, 2026 hearing on the federal scholarship tax credit rules, REG-117199-25, is now telephonic-only.
  • The proposal covers a non-refundable credit for qualified contributions to scholarship granting organizations.
  • Owners and advisers planning to comment in person should prepare to participate by phone instead.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Federal Scholarship Tax Credit; Hearing
