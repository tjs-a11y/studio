IRS Finalizes Retirement Plan Auto-Enrollment Rules; SBA Revises Two Programs

Final SECURE 2.0 auto-enrollment regulations arrive, unchecked W-9s can become 1099 headaches, and SBA updates two programs.

The IRS has issued final regulations on the automatic enrollment requirements under Section 414A. Employers that sponsor certain retirement plans should review how the rules apply to them.

Auto-Enrollment Rules Are Now Final

  • Final IRS regulations now govern the automatic enrollment requirements under Section 414A for certain retirement plans.
  • The rules carry out changes made by the SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022.
  • Certain cash or deferred arrangements must qualify as eligible automatic contribution arrangements that meet additional requirements.
  • Employers that maintain affected plans, along with participants and beneficiaries, fall within the rules' scope.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Automatic Enrollment Requirements Under Section 414A

Unchecked W-9s Can Trigger IRS Notices

  • A W-9 can be collected on time and filed properly yet still contain a mismatched name and TIN.
  • That bad pairing can carry over onto a Form 1099 issued to the vendor.
  • The result can be an IRS notice months later, so verifying vendor details before 1099 filing saves cleanup work.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: The W-9 Problem Your Busy Season Workflow Audit Can Miss

SBA Streamlines Surety Bond Guarantees

  • A final SBA rule reduces document submission and retention burdens for surety companies in the Surety Bond Guarantee program.
  • The rule fixes conflicting provisions in the program regulations.
  • It also revises outdated Preferred Surety admission requirements and the Quarterly Contract Completion Report.

Source: Federal Register: SBA: Rule: Surety Bond Guarantee Program: Streamlining and Modernizing

More Leverage for SBIC Funds

  • An SBA interim final rule implements the Investing in All of America Act of 2025 for the SBIC program.
  • It raises the maximum leverage available to SBICs issuing standard debentures, at ratios of up to two-to-one against private capital.

Source: Federal Register: SBA: Rule: Small Business Investment Company (SBIC) Leverage Availability
