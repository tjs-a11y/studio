IRS Finalizes Rules to Accept Card Tax Payments Without Third-Party Processors

New IRS card-payment rules, a Connecticut preparer charged over 4,335 returns, and state tax fights in Colorado, Washington and California.

Final IRS regulations let the agency accept credit and debit card tax payments directly, with no third-party processor in the middle.

IRS can take your card directly

  • Final regulations let the IRS accept credit and debit card tax payments itself, bypassing third-party processors.
  • The rules implement changes from the Taxpayer First Act, which could simplify how owners pay federal tax bills.
  • They also lift restrictions on the IRS paying fees under contracts tied to card-based tax payments.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

Preparer charged over 4,335 returns

  • 4,335 allegedly fraudulent returns were filed with the IRS through a Connecticut tax business, authorities say.
  • Former owner Yuridia Trujillo, 43, of Meriden, has been arrested.
  • The case is a reminder to verify who prepares your returns and review what gets filed.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Ex-Connecticut Tax Business Owner Accused of Filing 4,335 Fraudulent Returns

State tax fights heat up

  • Colorado: A Denver judge ruled Wednesday a graduated income tax measure can stay on the November ballot.
  • That measure would replace Colorado's flat rate, though the legal challenge against it continues.
  • Washington: Gov. Bob Ferguson warns repealing the new millionaires tax would make childcare less affordable.
  • California: Meta struck a stock deal to keep CEO Mark Zuckerberg in-state if a billionaire wealth tax passes.
