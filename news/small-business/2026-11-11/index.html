Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · Small Business Money Brief

Tax preparers must renew PTINs before Dec. 31; IRS updates two proposed rules

PTIN renewal season opens, opportunity zone reporting rules get corrections, and voters split on state taxes on the wealthy.

Current preparer tax identification numbers expire on Dec. 31, 2026. Anyone paid to prepare returns needs a renewed PTIN to keep filing in the new year.

PTIN renewal season is here

  • Current PTINs expire Dec. 31, 2026, so preparers face a year-end deadline.
  • Paid return preparers must hold a valid PTIN, and it must be renewed every year.
  • Bookkeepers who prepare client returns need a current number before the 2027 filing season.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Attention Tax Pros: It’s PTIN Renewal Time

IRS tweaks two proposed rules

  • The IRS corrected proposed opportunity zone reporting regulations (REG-116506-25), first published Sept. 11, 2026.
  • Those rules would require qualified opportunity funds to file information returns and send statements to investors who sell their stakes.
  • Qualified opportunity zone businesses would also have to send statements to the funds holding interests in them.
  • The Dec. 15, 2026 hearing on the scholarship tax credit rules (REG-117199-25) is now telephone-only, at 10 a.m. ET.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Information Reporting Regarding Qualified Opportunity Zones and Updated Qualified Opportunity Fund Certification and Decertification Procedures; Correction · Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Federal Scholarship Tax Credit; Hearing

Voters split on taxing the wealthy

  • About 52.9% of California voters rejected a one-time 5% wealth tax in preliminary results, versus 47.1% in favor.
  • Washington voters upheld a 9.9% tax on household incomes above $1 million.
  • The Washington tax still faces a major court challenge that could reshape the state's budget and tax policy.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Billionaire Tax Rejected by California Voters · CPA Practice Advisor: Voters Upheld Washington ‘Millionaires Tax.’ Will Courts Do the Same?

Fake IRS agent pleads guilty

  • Sandra Vega, 48, of Pecos, NM, pleaded guilty to four counts of wire fraud.
  • She also admitted to impersonating a federal officer and demanding something of value.
  • The case shows IRS impersonation schemes remain a live risk for business owners and their clients.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: New Mexico Woman Who Posed as IRS Agent Pleads Guilty to Federal Fraud Charges
