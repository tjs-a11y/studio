Payroll Tax Cap Climbs to $192,300 in 2027; IRS Finalizes Direct Card Payment Rules

The 2027 Social Security wage base rises, the IRS can take card payments directly, and dyed diesel penalties are paused through 2026.

The Social Security payroll tax cap rises from $184,500 to $192,300 in 2027. Update payroll setups and estimated tax plans before year-end.

Social Security wage base goes up

  • The payroll tax cap moves from $184,500 to $192,300 in 2027, so more high-end wages face Social Security tax.
  • Employers paying salaries above the old cap should update payroll systems before the first 2027 pay run.
  • Self-employed owners with large net earnings will owe Social Security tax on more income next year.
  • Benefits also get a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment, according to the Social Security Administration.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: Social Security’s Payroll Tax Cap is Going Up in 2027

IRS can take cards directly

  • Final IRS rules let the agency accept credit and debit card tax payments itself, without routing taxpayers to outside processors.
  • The rules implement Taxpayer First Act changes and lift limits on the IRS paying fees under card-payment contracts.
  • Card payment terms could change, so check costs before charging your next tax bill.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Rule: Regulations Regarding Payment of Tax by Commercially Acceptable Means

Dyed diesel penalties paused

  • The IRS is suspending federal tax penalties for selling or using dyed diesel on highways nationwide for the rest of 2026.
  • Trucking, farming and construction businesses gain temporary relief from a normally costly violation.

Source: CPA Practice Advisor: IRS Halts Penalties for Dyed Diesel Fuel Use for the Rest of 2026

Premium tax credit rules proposed

  • Proposed IRS regulations would change premium tax credit eligibility, how the credit is computed, and repayment of excess advance credits.
  • Freelancers buying Exchange health coverage should watch for changes that could affect their year-end tax bill.

Source: Federal Register: IRS: Proposed Rule: Premium Tax Credit
