    Rate con red flags, in plain English
    
    
    Detention
    Pay for waiting at a shipper or receiver past the free time (usually 2 hours). If the rate con has no detention rate, you may wait for free. Ask for a written hourly rate, when the clock starts, and how arrival is proven.
    
    
    TONU (truck ordered, not used)
    A flat fee when a broker cancels after you're dispatched or at the dock. "At broker discretion" means you may never see it. Ask for a fixed dollar amount.
    
    
    Lumper fees
    Fees paid to unloaders at the dock. Make sure the rate con says the broker reimburses them with a receipt, and how fast.
    
    
    Fines, deductions and chargebacks
    Penalties for late check calls, tracking gaps, missing paperwork or late delivery are often buried in the fine print and taken out of your pay. Know every dollar amount before you sign.
    
    
    Setoff (offset) clauses
    Lets a broker take money owed on one load from your pay on another, including disputed claims. It's a common way carriers lose pay they already earned.
    
    
    Payment terms
    Net 30 means you're paid 30 days after paperwork is received. Net 45 or longer squeezes cash flow, and quick-pay fees eat into the rate.
    
    
    Broker identity and fraud
    Check the MC/DOT number on FMCSA's public record before you haul. A broker emailing from a free address (Gmail, Yahoo) is a common fraud warning sign.
