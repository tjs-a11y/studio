How to Check If Your Oil and Gas Royalty Check Is Correct

To check an oil and gas royalty check, verify your net mineral acres, the lease royalty fraction, the spacing or pooled unit acreage, the decimal shown on the statement, and each production, price, tax, and deduction line.

How do net mineral acres, lease fraction and unit acreage affect the decimal?

For a simple proportional-interest example, an expected royalty decimal can be estimated as:

Net mineral acres × lease royalty fraction ÷ unit acreage = estimated royalty decimal.

For example, 40 net mineral acres × a 0.1875 lease royalty fraction ÷ a 640-acre unit = 0.01171875. This is an illustration only; actual ownership and allocation terms can change the calculation.

What should you verify first?

  • Net mineral acres: Check deeds, probate or title records, division orders, and operator records. Confirm net acres attributed to your interest, not just gross tract acreage.
  • Lease royalty fraction: Read the executed lease and amendments. Convert a fraction such as 3/16 to 0.1875 only after confirming it applies to your interest.
  • Spacing or pooled unit acreage: Verify the actual unit acreage used for allocation. Do not assume every well uses 640 acres or that a permit's acreage proves your payable interest. Check pooling documents, unit designation, and the operator's allocation basis.
  • Reported owner decimal: Compare the statement or division-order decimal with your calculation. Ask which unit, tract participation factor, and ownership fraction the operator used.
  • Production and price: Check reported volumes, product type, price per unit, and statement period. Timing, adjustments, and prior-period corrections can change payments.
  • Deductions and taxes: Identify each line and its stated basis. Compare deductions with the lease, applicable law, and operator explanation; do not assume a deduction is permitted or prohibited without reviewing the terms.

What if the decimal or payment does not match?

  1. Save the statement, division order, lease and amendments, ownership records, and unit or pooling documents.
  2. Recalculate the expected decimal using verified inputs and record each assumption.
  3. Ask the operator in writing for the acreage basis, decimal calculation, allocation method, production detail, and explanation for each deduction or adjustment.
  4. Compare statements across periods to identify changes in decimal, unit, price, production, or deductions.
  5. If the difference remains unexplained, consider an independent landman or qualified oil-and-gas attorney familiar with the state and lease.

Important limitations

The basic formula may not capture tract participation factors, nonparticipating interests, overriding royalties, depth or formation limits, multiple leases, unit changes, title issues, or other contract-specific allocation terms. Unit acreage and a lease fraction alone do not prove a payment is correct. Verify governing documents and ask the operator for its calculation.
