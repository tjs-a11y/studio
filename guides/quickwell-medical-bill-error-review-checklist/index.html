Bill Review

Medical Bill Error Review Checklist

Organize a medical bill review by checking duplicate charges, dates, insurance adjustments, and unexplained line items.

An unexpected bill deserves a calm, line-by-line review before you assume the amount is correct. Compare the statement with the service period, prior bills, usage or service records, adjustments, and the provider's written explanation.

A practical review process

  1. Confirm the account, service dates, billing period, due date, and whether the statement is final, corrected, or estimated.
  2. Compare like with like: days, meter units, plan, rate, or service category. A larger total does not automatically mean the price increased.
  3. Mark unusual line items such as duplicates, changed rates, unexplained fees, missing credits, or charges outside the service period.
  4. Gather supporting records: meter readings, explanation-of-benefits documents where relevant, prior statements, payment confirmations, and correspondence.
  5. Contact the billing department with specific questions. Ask for an itemized explanation and confirmation of any payment or appeal deadline.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming a higher total proves a rate increase when usage or billing days may have changed
  • Missing a provider's appeal deadline while investigating
  • Putting medical or account identifiers into an unsecured planning document

Records worth keeping

Keep statements, service-period comparisons, usage records, relevant benefit statements, payment confirmations, and dated provider responses.

Store sensitive account, health, identity, or customer records only in an appropriate secure system. This guide is general information, not a substitute for qualified legal, medical, financial, or safety advice.
