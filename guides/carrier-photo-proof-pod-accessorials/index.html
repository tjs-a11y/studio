Carrier photo evidence: document pickup, delivery, damage and accessorials

Carrier operations guide · Updated 30 October 2026

When a delivery, cargo-condition or accessorial question comes up days later, a consistent record helps reconstruct what happened. Create evidence at the time of the event, not after a dispute starts.

Capture the event, not just the load

  • Photograph the freight and visible condition before loading when permitted; capture seals, trailer condition and relevant exceptions.
  • At delivery, record visible damage, shortages, refused freight or other exceptions before leaving the site.
  • For detention or layover, keep arrival/departure timestamps and supporting facility or dispatch records.

Keep the supporting documents

  • Match each photo set to the load number, date, location and relevant rate confirmation.
  • Keep signed bills of lading, PODs, exception notations, lumper receipts and written broker/shipper instructions.
  • Preserve original files. A photo fingerprint can help show a file has not changed, but it does not independently prove when, where or by whom a photo was taken.

Create a local record

  • Proof creates a timestamp and file fingerprint you keep. Use it as one part of a broader documentation process, not as a substitute for a signed POD, witness, ELD record or facility receipt.

Use Proof

Proof can help with this workflow. Review the source records and verify the result before making a business, safety, tax or compliance decision.

Open Proof

Follow shipper/receiver rules and applicable privacy and recording laws. Do not photograph people, restricted areas or confidential cargo information without authorization. A file fingerprint is not independent authentication of the event.
