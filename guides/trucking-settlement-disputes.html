What to Do If a Freight Broker Short Pays Your Settlement

If a freight broker appears to short pay your settlement, compare the signed rate confirmation with the proof of delivery and final remittance, calculate the exact variance, and request a written explanation with supporting documents.

What documents should you reconcile?

  • Signed rate confirmation: Confirm linehaul, fuel surcharge, accessorial rates, approval requirements, and written amendments.
  • Proof of delivery (POD): Verify the load reference, delivery date, signature, and relevant notations.
  • Accessorial evidence: Gather detention timestamps, layover approvals, lumper receipts, truck-ordered-not-used communications, and proof required by the rate confirmation.
  • Final settlement or remittance advice: Record each payment line, deduction, adjustment, and stated reason. A blank field does not establish that an amount is zero or approved.
  • Factoring statement: Reconcile broker gross pay separately from factor fees, reserves, and adjustments. A factoring fee is not automatically a broker short payment.

How do you calculate the difference?

Add the agreed linehaul, fuel surcharge, and supported accessorials. Compare that expected gross amount with the broker's reported gross settlement, then list each unexplained difference separately. Keep broker-side deductions separate from factoring charges and contract-authorized amounts.

How should you dispute the discrepancy?

  1. Make a one-page reconciliation showing the load number, agreed amount, paid amount, variance, and supporting document for each line.
  2. Email the broker's payment or carrier-relations contact with the disputed amount and attach the rate confirmation, POD, receipts, and relevant timestamps.
  3. Ask the broker to identify the contract term and evidence supporting any deduction, and request a corrected remittance or written explanation by a reasonable date.
  4. Keep original files, emails, call notes, and proof of submission; follow any notice and dispute deadlines in your agreement.

A numerical difference is a reason to investigate, not by itself proof of unlawful withholding. Contract terms, accessorial approval rules, payment records, and applicable law determine the next step.
