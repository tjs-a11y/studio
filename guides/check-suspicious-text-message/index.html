How to check a suspicious text message safely

Consumer guide · Updated 17 November 2026

Start by not replying or clicking. The FTC's May 2026 consumer alert says text messages were the most-reported way scammers contacted people in 2025. Its April 2025 alert describes fake bank fraud alerts, delivery notices, unpaid-toll messages, job offers and wrong-number texts. Those are warning patterns, not proof that any particular sender is fraudulent.

Quickwell · Consumer safety checklist

Unexpected text? Pause before you click.

Use these steps before responding to a delivery notice, bank alert, toll message, job offer or other unexpected text.

  1. Do not reply or tap the link. An unexpected message may be designed to make you react quickly.
  2. Do not use the message's contact details. Open the official app yourself, type a website address you already know, or use a phone number from a statement or payment card.
  3. Verify the claim independently. A familiar sender name or logo is not proof. Contact the company, bank or agency through a channel you already trust.
  4. Keep sensitive information out of the reply. Do not send account, payment or identity details in response to an unexpected message.
  5. Report and delete. Use your messaging app's report-junk option or forward unwanted texts to 7726 (SPAM). Report suspected fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Verify through a channel you choose

Do not use the link or phone number in the unexpected message. Open the organization's official app yourself, type a website address you already know, or call a number from a statement or payment card. The FTC advises checking with the company, bank or agency through contact details you know are real. A familiar sender name or logo does not establish that the message is genuine.

Look for pressure and unexpected requests

Fake fraud alerts, delivery notices, unpaid-toll demands, job offers and wrong-number texts are among the examples the FTC has described. Slow down when a message creates urgency, asks you to click a link, or requests personal or financial information. A warning pattern is a reason to verify independently, not a verdict about who sent the message.

Report unwanted or fraudulent texts

The FTC says you can use your phone's report-junk option, forward unwanted texts to 7726 (SPAM), report them in your messaging app, and report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. After reporting, delete the message. If you need a record, keep a screenshot privately; do not post account details or other sensitive information publicly.
