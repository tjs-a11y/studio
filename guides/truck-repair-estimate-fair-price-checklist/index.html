Truck repair estimate checklist: parts, labor, diagnostics and authorization

Carrier operations guide · Updated 30 October 2026

A low truck-repair quote is not always the lowest final bill. Before authorizing work, make sure the estimate explains the fault, the repair, the parts and what happens if the diagnosis changes.

Ask for an itemized estimate

  • Vehicle identification, odometer, reported symptoms and diagnostic findings.
  • Labor hours and rate, parts descriptions and part numbers, shop supplies, towing, taxes and diagnostic fees.
  • Separate required repairs from recommended maintenance and explain safety-critical items.

Control changes before work begins

  • Ask the shop to call before exceeding the estimate or replacing additional parts.
  • Get the old-parts return policy, parts/labor warranty and expected completion date in writing.
  • For a second opinion, compare the actual repair scope—not just the grand total.

Check the estimate

  • Paste the estimate into Fair Price to identify unclear line items, missing detail and questions to ask.
  • The tool is a review aid; it does not independently verify local labor rates, diagnose the truck or certify a repair.
