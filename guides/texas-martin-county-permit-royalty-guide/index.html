Martin County, Texas: how to follow permits and check a royalty statement

County research guide · Updated 30 October 2026

If you own or are researching mineral interests in Martin County, use permit activity as a starting point—not as proof that a well is producing or that your tract earns royalties.

What the recent public data says

In Quickwell's October 30, 2026 snapshot of the Texas Railroad Commission's daily drilling-permit file, Martin County had 93 unique approved permits issued from October 1–29, after repeated permit-number versions were consolidated. This is a dated permit count, not a count of producing wells, mineral owners, leases, or royalty payors.

How to check a permit against your tract

  1. Search the RRC Drilling Permit (W-1) records by county, operator, lease or permit number.
  2. Open the permit record and plat. Compare the survey, abstract, lease/unit acreage and well location with your own deed, lease and tract description.
  3. Check later records for spud, completion and reported production. A permit alone does not establish that drilling occurred or that production began.
  4. If your tract may be in a pooled unit, compare the recorded unit information and your lease terms; obtain title or legal help where the description is unclear.

Open Permit Watch — select Martin County and follow its free county RSS feed to monitor new public permit records. Always confirm a record in the official RRC query.

If you already receive royalty statements

Use your lease and division order—not the county permit count—to establish the royalty fraction and ownership decimal. Check the statement's property/lease identifier, sales month, volume, price, taxes, deductions and decimal interest. A decimal difference is a reason to verify the inputs and ask the payor for an explanation; it is not, by itself, proof of an underpayment.

Recalculate your royalty decimal

Royalty Check compares a decimal calculated from net mineral acres, unit acres and lease royalty fraction with the decimal printed on your statement. It cannot establish title, confirm that your tract is in a unit, or determine whether deductions are permitted.

Official records to keep together

  • Your deed, probate or other title documents and any lease or amendments.
  • RRC permit number, W-1 plat and relevant well/lease identifiers.
  • Division order and royalty statements for the same property and sales months.
  • Written questions and responses from the operator/payor.
