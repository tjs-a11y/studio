Freight Audit

Detention and Layover Pay Audit Checklist

Organize arrival, departure, and supporting records to review detention, layover, and other accessorial pay.

Freight paperwork only protects a carrier when the agreed rate, shipment records, service terms, accessorial rules, and settlement reconcile. Review the load as one transaction instead of treating each document as a separate task.

A practical review process

  1. Match the signed rate confirmation to the dispatched load: parties, load number, linehaul, fuel surcharge, appointments, and payment terms.
  2. Compare the BOL and signed POD with the confirmation. Check locations, dates, reference numbers, quantities, and written changes made in transit.
  3. Separate linehaul from accessorials. For detention, layover, truck ordered not used, lumper, tolls, or other charges, identify the contract rule and supporting records.
  4. Reconcile the settlement line by line. Record each deduction, amount, stated reason, and the document or agreement clause supporting it.
  5. Build one evidence packet with the confirmation, BOL, POD, timestamps, receipts, dispatch messages, and a concise written question for each unresolved amount.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Relying on a verbal promise instead of a written rate-confirmation change
  • Requesting accessorial pay without the required arrival, departure, notice, or receipt evidence
  • Comparing gross revenue with net settlement without separating operating expenses

Records worth keeping

Keep the rate confirmation, BOL, signed POD, dispatch messages, timestamps, receipts, settlement statement, and revised written terms together.
