Consumer Savings

Subscription Price Increase Audit

Review recurring subscription price changes, renewal dates, and cancellation terms to identify avoidable monthly costs.

A small recurring price change or misleading package comparison can quietly affect a household budget. Use a consistent unit, check renewal terms, and distinguish a real saving from a temporary promotion.

A practical review process

  1. Write down the current price, package size or billing period, and the date the amount takes effect.
  2. Compare the same unit: price per ounce, pound, item, or serving; for subscriptions, compare full monthly and annual cost.
  3. Separate regular pricing from introductory offers, coupons, delivery fees, taxes, and automatic renewal charges.
  4. Check alternatives before switching. Review quality, quantity, cancellation terms, shipping, and whether a lower price requires a larger commitment.
  5. Record the decision and set a review date before a promotion or renewal ends.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing different package sizes using shelf price alone
  • Treating an introductory rate as the long-term price
  • Canceling before checking data export, access loss, or contract conditions

Records worth keeping

Keep dated price notices or receipts, package quantities, subscription terms, renewal dates, cancellation confirmation, and the comparison calculation.
