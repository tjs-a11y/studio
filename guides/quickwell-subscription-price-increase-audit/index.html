Subscription price increase audit: find and challenge higher charges

Updated 9 November 2026 · Household money checklist

A subscription can become more expensive without looking like a new purchase: a promotional rate ends, a plan changes, tax or add-ons appear, or the same service is billed twice. Compare the actual transactions first, then verify the provider's notice, plan and cancellation terms before deciding what to do.

Find the recurring charge pattern

Download a useful transaction history from your bank or card provider—often 6 to 13 months is enough to spot monthly and annual renewals. Work from the transaction date, merchant descriptor and amount. Do not assume every similar merchant name is the same subscription; payment processors and parent companies can appear under different descriptors.

  • Group transactions by merchant descriptor and likely billing cadence.
  • Compare the latest amount with the previous 3–6 charges.
  • Flag a new amount, a second charge in the same period, or a renewal you do not recognize.
  • Check annual charges as well as monthly charges; an annual renewal can be missed in a short review window.
  • Separate refunds, reversals, taxes and one-time purchases from the recurring base price.

Calculate the change correctly

Compare like with like: same service, plan, billing period and currency. If a monthly subscription moved from $12 to $15, the increase is $3 per month, or 25%. The annualized difference is $36 if the new price stays in place for 12 months. If the plan also added users or features, identify that change rather than treating the entire difference as an unexplained increase.

Check the account and the notice

  1. Sign in by typing the provider's known address or using its official app—do not follow an unexpected billing email link.
  2. Open billing history and plan details. Record the old plan, new plan, effective date, amount, taxes and add-ons.
  3. Search your email for the provider name and terms such as “price change,” “renewal,” “plan update” and “receipt.” Check spam and archived mail.
  4. Read the contract and cancellation policy for notice timing, renewal date, refund limits and any minimum term.
  5. If the change is unclear, contact the provider using contact details from its official site or account portal. Ask when and how the price change was disclosed and request the record of your consent or plan selection if relevant.

Choose the right next step

If you still want the service, ask whether the old plan is available, whether a lower tier meets your needs, or whether an annual plan is genuinely cheaper after comparing the total cost and cancellation terms. If you want to leave, follow the provider's stated cancellation process and save the confirmation number, email or screenshot. Check the next statement to confirm billing stopped.

If you do not recognize the charge or believe it is an error, contact the merchant promptly and contact your bank or card issuer about its dispute process and deadlines. Do not assume cancelling the subscription automatically disputes a past charge, or that a card dispute automatically cancels the service contract.

Keep a small audit log

For each service, note the merchant descriptor, plan, old and new price, date the change took effect, notice found, cancellation deadline, action taken and confirmation reference. This prevents the same issue from resurfacing at the next renewal.
