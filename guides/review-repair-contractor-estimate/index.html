How to review a repair or contractor estimate

Consumer guide · Updated 31 October 2026

To review a repair or contractor estimate, compare the written scope, parts or materials, labor, fees, exclusions, timing and approval process—not just the final total. Ask for clarification in writing and compare another estimate when the work is expensive or difficult to assess.

Check the estimate line by line

  • Scope: identify the exact work, quantities and completion steps.
  • Parts and materials: check descriptions, quantities and whether replacements are new, used or reconditioned.
  • Labor: ask for hours, rates or the method used to calculate the charge.
  • Extra fees: ask what diagnostic, shop-supply, disposal, delivery or permit charges cover.
  • Approval: ask when the provider must contact you before adding work or exceeding the estimate.
  • Timing and payment: confirm start date, completion estimate, deposit and milestones.
  • Warranty: ask what is covered, for how long and what is excluded.

For auto repairs, request a written estimate

The FTC's Auto Repair Basics guide says a written estimate should identify the condition to be repaired, the parts needed and anticipated labor charges. It should also state that the shop will contact you for approval before work exceeds a specified amount of time or money; state law may require this. The FTC suggests shopping around and considering a second opinion for expensive or complicated repairs.

For home projects, compare the same scope

Ask each contractor to describe the work, materials, completion date, total price and exclusions in writing. Licensing, deposit, permit and change-order rules vary by state and locality, so confirm local requirements with the relevant government office.

Warnings are questions, not verdicts

A vague fee or high total is a reason to ask for detail, not proof of fraud or an unlawful charge. A meaningful comparison depends on location, scope, parts, labor, access, timing, warranty and contract terms.
