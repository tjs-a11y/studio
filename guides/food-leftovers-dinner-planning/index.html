How long do cooked leftovers last in the refrigerator?

FoodSafety.gov advises refrigerating perishable food within 2 hours (within 1 hour above 90°F), cooling it in shallow containers, keeping the refrigerator at 40°F or below, using cooked leftovers within 4 days, and reheating them to 165°F.

Reviewed October 31, 2026 · Sources listed below

Start with the clock and the refrigerator, not the recipe. FoodSafety.gov says perishable food should be refrigerated within 2 hours after cooking, serving, purchase or delivery. If the air temperature is above 90°F, the limit is 1 hour. Put leftovers into shallow containers so they cool promptly, keep the refrigerator at 40°F or below, use cooked leftovers within 4 days, and reheat leftovers to 165°F.

A quick leftover safety checklist

  • Check the time. If perishable food sat at room temperature for more than 2 hours, discard it. Use the 1-hour limit when the temperature is above 90°F, such as a hot car or outdoor gathering.
  • Cool it promptly. Divide large portions into shallow containers instead of leaving a large pot on the counter to cool slowly.
  • Keep the refrigerator cold. FoodSafety.gov recommends 40°F or below. An inexpensive appliance thermometer is more reliable than guessing from the dial.
  • Label the date. Write the cooking or storage date on the container so the household can use older leftovers first.
  • Use the four-day window. FoodSafety.gov says to use cooked leftovers within 4 days. Freeze portions you will not use in that period rather than leaving them in the refrigerator.
  • Reheat thoroughly. Reheat leftovers to 165°F. Stir or rotate food when using a microwave so cold spots do not remain.

Plan a meal around what is safe to use

Once you have checked storage time and handling, look at the ingredients that need using first. A container of cooked vegetables might become a frittata filling, a grain bowl, soup or a pasta sauce. Cooked chicken can be added to tacos, a salad or a quick skillet meal. Keep the meal idea separate from the safety decision: a recipe suggestion cannot make food safe if it was stored outside the recommended time or temperature limits.

For a more specific storage question, use the FoodSafety.gov cold-storage chart for the food in front of you. Different foods and preparations can have different storage recommendations. Follow package directions and any instructions from the person who prepared the food, especially when the food is intended for someone at higher risk of foodborne illness.
