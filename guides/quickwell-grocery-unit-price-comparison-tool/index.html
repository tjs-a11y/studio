Grocery unit price calculator: compare cost per ounce, pound or item

The shelf price tells you what you pay today. The unit price tells you how much you pay for the same amount of product. Compare unit prices before assuming a larger package or sale sticker is the better value.

To calculate a unit price, divide the package price by the quantity in the same unit: unit price = total price ÷ quantity. Use the same measurement for both products—for example, dollars per ounce for two packages of cereal.

Worked example: which cereal box costs less?

The Illinois Department of Agriculture gives a useful comparison: a 14-ounce box for $2.52 costs 18 cents per ounce; a 20-ounce box for $3.00 costs 15 cents per ounce; and a 32-ounce box for $5.12 costs 16 cents per ounce. In that example, the 20-ounce box—not the largest box—is the lowest cost per ounce.

Five checks before choosing the lower unit price

  1. Compare like with like. Use the same unit and compare similar products. For weight, convert pounds to ounces when needed; one pound is 16 ounces.
  2. Check the shelf label. Look for the unit price printed near the total price. If it is missing or looks wrong, calculate it from the package price and net quantity.
  3. Account for coupons. Subtract a coupon that applies to the item before dividing, and compare the resulting price per unit.
  4. Consider waste and storage. A bulk package is not a saving if you cannot use it before it spoils or you have nowhere to store it.
  5. Compare quality and usable quantity. A lower unit price is useful only when the products are sufficiently similar for your needs.

Unit pricing makes differently sized packages easier to compare when the same unit is used. NIST's 2025 guide describes best practices for clear, consistent unit-price presentation; it is not a substitute for applicable state requirements.
