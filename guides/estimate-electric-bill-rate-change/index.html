How to estimate your electric bill after a rate change

Consumer guide · Updated 31 October 2026

To estimate an electric bill after a rate change, start with the kilowatt-hours (kWh) you expect to use, multiply by the applicable per-kWh energy rate, then add delivery charges, fixed fees, taxes and other line items that apply to your account. A new supply rate does not necessarily change every part of the bill.

Collect the figures from your latest bill

  • Billing period start/end dates and number of days.
  • Total electricity usage in kWh.
  • Supply or energy rate per kWh and the fixed-rate expiration date.
  • Delivery charges, separated into per-kWh and fixed monthly amounts.
  • Taxes, riders, adjustments, credits and other fees.
  • Plan type: fixed, variable, tiered or time-of-use, plus any minimum-use or cancellation terms.

Build a transparent estimate

A simple starting point is expected kWh × applicable energy rate. Add the other bill components using the tariff or supplier plan that applies to the account. If the rate changes during a billing cycle, split the estimate across the old and new periods when the plan terms require it. If usage changes by season, compare similar months or use your own recent usage history rather than assuming every month is identical.

Average retail prices are not your tariff

The U.S. Energy Information Administration publishes average retail electricity prices, but it explains that these averages are not the same as the rates applied to an individual customer's services. Actual charges depend on the utility or supplier, customer class, rate schedule, usage level and other bill components. Use the tariff or plan document that applies to your account for an account-level estimate.

Before a fixed rate expires

  • Confirm the exact expiration date and the rate that applies afterward.
  • Compare the full price structure, not only the advertised energy rate.
  • Check monthly fees, minimum usage rules, peak windows and cancellation charges.
  • Compare offers using the same estimated usage and billing period.
  • Keep the bill and plan documents used in the comparison.
