How to find and cancel recurring charges in bank or card statements

Find recurring charges by reviewing several months of bank and card statements, matching repeated merchant names and amounts, then cancel directly with the company and keep a copy of the cancellation request.

Reviewed October 31, 2026 · Sources listed below

Collect the records before you start

  • Download at least three months of checking-account and credit-card activity; six to twelve months makes annual renewals easier to spot.
  • Include the accounts and cards you actually use, but never give a review tool your bank login, password, PIN or one-time security code.
  • Keep original statements unchanged. Work from a copy so you can return to the bank record if a merchant name is abbreviated.
  • Record the merchant descriptor, amount, posting date, payment method and any matching receipt or confirmation email.

Check each likely recurring payment

Look for the same merchant appearing on a similar date each month or year. Compare amounts as well as names: some subscriptions change price, add tax or bill through a payment processor with a different descriptor. Look for free trials that became paid plans, duplicate subscriptions across family members, annual renewals, extra seats and fees attached to an account you no longer use.

Do not assume two similar charges are duplicates. A household may have two separate accounts, a merchant may split a purchase into installments, or a payment processor may use a different label from the service name. Open the merchant account or use a receipt to confirm the service, plan, billing period and cancellation terms.

Cancel with the company and keep evidence

  1. Find the company that operates the subscription and read its current cancellation instructions.
  2. Follow the stated cancellation process. If you cancel by phone or chat, note the date, time and case number where available.
  3. Save the cancellation confirmation, email or screenshot, and any terms that explain when billing stops.
  4. Check the next statement. Confirm whether a final charge is consistent with the terms.
  5. If you believe a charge was unauthorized and the company will not resolve it, contact your bank or card issuer promptly and ask about its dispute process and deadlines.

The FTC recommends looking up the company, following its cancellation instructions, keeping a copy of the request and monitoring bank or credit-card statements. It also advises consumers to dispute charges with their card company when they were charged without consent and the business will not refund them. Payment method, contract terms and applicable law can affect the available options.
