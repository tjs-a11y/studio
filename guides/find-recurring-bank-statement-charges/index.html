How to find recurring charges in a bank statement

Consumer guide · Updated 31 October 2026

To find recurring charges in a bank statement, review several statement periods, group transactions by merchant and amount, and verify each repeated charge against the subscription, authorization or service agreement before cancelling or disputing it. A repeated amount is a lead to investigate, not proof that a charge is unauthorized.

Review transactions systematically

  • Collect two or more statement periods so monthly and annual patterns are easier to spot.
  • Group merchant names; payment processors and shortened descriptors can make one vendor appear under different labels.
  • Compare dates and amounts for monthly, quarterly and annual patterns, including small increases.
  • Compare duplicate-looking entries by posting date, amount and descriptor before disputing.
  • Match each charge to the invoice, free-trial terms, renewal notice, contract or cancellation confirmation.
  • Set unfamiliar charges aside for verification through the bank's official channel.

Separate cancelling a service from stopping a debit

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that a consumer can revoke permission for a company to take automatic payments from a bank account and notify both the company and the bank. But stopping an automatic payment does not itself cancel the underlying contract or erase an amount still owed. Follow the service's cancellation process, keep written confirmation, and arrange another payment method for any remaining obligation.

Keep evidence before taking action

  • Save the statement line, invoice, contract and cancellation confirmation.
  • Record the date and method used to contact the merchant and bank.
  • Check later statements to confirm whether billing stopped.
  • Use the bank's official dispute process for a charge you did not authorize or a payment that continued after a valid stop instruction.

The FTC also recommends keeping a copy of subscription cancellation requests and monitoring later statements. The process can differ for a debit card, credit card, ACH debit or contract dispute.
