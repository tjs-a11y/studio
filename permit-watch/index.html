      About the data

      Where does this come from?

      The Railroad Commission of Texas (RRC) publishes its drilling permit data as free downloadable files. We download the RRC's daily drilling permit file once a day, keep the last 30 days, and show it here. Each permit links to the RRC's own Drilling Permit (W-1) Query, where you can search the permit number to see the official record and plats.

      How fresh is it?

      We update once a day, in the morning (Texas time). Data may lag the RRC's site by up to a day, and our history starts the day this tool began collecting it. If an RRC file is late or changes format, an update can be skipped; the date at the top shows when the data was last refreshed.

      Why does a permit say "Pending" or have no location?

      Some records in the RRC file are applications not yet approved, and some have no surface coordinates yet. Survey, abstract and API fields are only shown when the file provides them in a form we can read. Always check the official RRC record.

      What does a permit mean for a mineral owner?

      A drilling permit (Form W-1) means an operator has applied for, or received, approval to drill, re-enter or recomplete a well at a location. It doesn't mean a well will be drilled. Texas law requires notice to the surface owner, not to mineral owners who don't own the surface, which is why many mineral owners watch permits themselves.
