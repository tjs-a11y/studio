Calendar Spreads in VIX Backwardation

Calendar Spread VIX Backwardation

A calendar spread profits from the different speeds at which near- and far-dated options lose value. In VIX backwardation — when short-term volatility spikes above long-term — the front leg is richly priced to sell.

You expect near-term volatility, now higher than longer-term, to settle back down.

Pillar 1 · Illustrative setup

How this example is built

Sell a short-dated option and buy a longer-dated option at the same strike. The near leg decays faster than the far leg, which is where the profit comes from.

Illustrative inputs only — not live prices.

Sell front-month option~7 days, strike 32
Buy back-month option~45 days, same strike
Illustrative net debit~$1.45 / share

Pillar 2 · When & why

Why this fits VIX Backwardation

The thesis is mean reversion: during panic the term structure inverts (backwardation), and a calendar sells the inflated front month against a cheaper back month, profiting as volatility normalizes and the front leg decays.

This is a two-expiration position: its value at the front expiration depends on the back-month option's price then, which is driven by implied volatility — so there's no fixed max-profit number. Your capital at risk is the net debit paid; a continued volatility spike or a large directional move can work against it.
