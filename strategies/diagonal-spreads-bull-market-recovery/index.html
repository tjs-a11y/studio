Diagonal Spreads for Recovery

Diagonal Spread Gradual Recovery Phases

A diagonal spread blends a calendar and a vertical: a long back-month call gives bullish exposure while repeatedly sold front-month calls bring in income as a stock drifts higher.

You expect a slow, grinding recovery higher rather than a sharp spike.

Pillar 1 · Illustrative setup

How this example is built

Buy a longer-dated call at a lower strike and sell a shorter-dated call at a higher strike — part directional bet, part income from the decaying front call.

Illustrative inputs only — not live prices.

Buy back-month call (lower strike)195, ~60 days
Sell front-month call (higher strike)208, ~14 days

Pillar 2 · When & why

Why this fits Gradual Recovery Phases

Suited to a slow bullish turn. The plan is to let each front-month call decay toward worthless, then sell another ('rolling up' the strike as price rises), while the long back-month leg gains value as the stock recovers.

A two-expiration position again: the payoff when the front call expires depends on the back-month call's remaining value, set by volatility and time, so no single max-profit figure applies. Capital at risk is the net debit; a fast spike can see the short call run past you before you roll.
