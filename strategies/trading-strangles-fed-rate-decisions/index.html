Long Strangle for Macro Rate Decisions

Long Strangle Macro Rate Decisions

A long strangle is a lower-cost cousin of the straddle: you buy an OTM call and an OTM put, pay less, and need a bigger move to profit. Loss is capped at the premium paid.

You expect a large move but not the direction, and want a cheaper entry than a straddle.

Pillar 1 · Illustrative setup

How this example is built

Buy an out-of-the-money call and an out-of-the-money put. Both cost less than at-the-money options, so the entry is cheaper — but the underlying has to travel further to pay off.

Pillar 2 · When & why

Why this fits Macro Rate Decisions

Used around macro events — an FOMC rate decision or a major data release — that can reprice a whole index. The lower entry cost is the appeal; the trade-off is wider breakevens, so a moderate move can still lose.

Maximum loss is the premium paid, in the zone between the two strikes. Because both options start out-of-the-money, a move that isn't big enough leaves both worthless.
