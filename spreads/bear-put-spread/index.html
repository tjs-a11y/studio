Bear Put Spread

Put debit spread

A bear put spread is a defined-risk, debit strategy for a bearish view — cheaper than buying a put outright, with a maximum profit capped at the lower strike.

Bearish — you expect the stock to fall toward or past the lower strike.

How it is built

Buy the higher-strike put and sell the lower-strike put in the same expiration. You pay a net debit; the short put offsets part of the long put's cost and caps the downside profit.

The defined-risk math

Breakeven = long strike − net debit. Maximum profit = width − net debit; maximum loss = the net debit. Figures are per one spread (×100 shares).

When it is used, and the risk

Used when you are bearish but want to reduce the cost and time-decay drag of a long put, accepting a capped profit in return.

The maximum loss is the debit paid, realized if the stock closes above the higher strike. Like all debit spreads, it needs the move to happen before expiration.
