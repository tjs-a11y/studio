Bear Call Spread

Call credit spread

A bear call spread is the bearish mirror of the bull put spread: a defined-risk credit position that profits when the stock stays below your short strike.

Neutral-to-bearish — you expect the stock to stay below the lower strike.

How it is built

Sell the lower-strike call and buy the higher-strike call in the same expiration. You open for a net credit because the sold call is worth more than the protective long call.

The defined-risk math

Spread width5 ($500.00 per spread)
PositionCredit — you receive $100.00 per spread
Maximum profit$100.00
Maximum loss$400.00
Breakeven51

Breakeven = short strike + net credit. Maximum loss = width − net credit; maximum profit = the net credit. Figures are per one spread (×100 shares).

When it is used, and the risk

Used when you are mildly bearish or neutral and want to collect premium with a capped loss, rather than shorting stock or selling a naked call with unlimited risk.

The maximum loss, which is the width minus the credit, occurs if the stock closes above the higher strike. A strong rally is the risk; sideways or down is where you want it.
