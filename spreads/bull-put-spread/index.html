Bull Put Spread

Put credit spread

A bull put spread is a defined-risk, credit strategy used when you think a stock will hold above a level. You keep the credit if it does, and your loss is capped by the long put if it doesn't.

Neutral-to-bullish — you expect the stock to stay above the higher strike.

How it is built

Sell the higher-strike put and buy the lower-strike put in the same expiration. The sold put brings in more premium than the bought put costs, so you open for a net credit.

The defined-risk math

Breakeven = short strike − net credit. Maximum loss = width − net credit; maximum profit = the net credit. Figures are per one spread (×100 shares).

When it is used, and the risk

Traders reach for it when they are mildly bullish or just not bearish, want to be paid up front, and want a known worst case instead of the open-ended risk of selling a naked put.

The maximum loss is larger than the credit you collect, and it happens if the stock falls below the lower strike at expiration. The spread is still a short-premium position: time decay helps you, a sharp drop hurts you.
