Roadside Medical SOS Kit

If you can't speak, your car can.

Three things that help in a roadside medical emergency: a printable card for your glovebox and visor, your phone's real hands-free SOS turned on, and a one-tap screen that shows exactly where you are. It all runs on your device — nothing you type is uploaded.

1. Build your glovebox card

Fill in what an EMT or a bystander would need if you couldn't tell them. It saves on this device only, so you can reprint it anytime.

2. The one-tap "I need help now" screen

For when you can still tap but can't explain where you are. It reads your live GPS on this device and shows it big, with a script to read to the dispatcher. It does not place the call — tap 911 (or your local number) yourself, or use your phone's SOS below.

This tool does not call anyone and cannot summon help by itself. In a real emergency call your local emergency number (911 in the US) or use the phone SOS below. A web page can't dial for you or keep listening when your screen is off.

3. Arm your phone's real hands-free SOS

Your phone already has an SOS that works with the screen locked and your voice — set it up once. This is the part that actually reaches help.

  1. Say "Siri, call 911" — on iOS 17 and later you can drop "Hey". Set up Siri first in Settings → Siri.
  2. Turn on Emergency SOS: Settings → Emergency SOS → Call with Hold and Release (and Call with 5 Presses).
  3. Turn on Crash Detection: Settings → Emergency SOS → scroll to Crash Detection → on.
  4. Fill in Medical ID and turn on Share During Emergency Call: Health app → your photo → Medical ID.
  5. Add emergency contacts in Medical ID so they're texted your location when SOS runs.
  1. Say "Hey Google, call 911" after setting up Google Assistant.
  2. Turn on Emergency SOS: Settings → Safety & emergency → Emergency SOS (often 5 presses of the power button).
  3. Turn on car crash detection where available (Personal Safety app, Pixel and some others).
  4. Fill in Medical information and emergency contacts: Settings → Safety & emergency.
  5. Turn on Emergency Location Service and location sharing to a trusted contact.
Steps vary by model and software version. If a menu name differs, search your phone's Settings for "emergency" or "SOS". Test nothing by actually calling 911 — read the steps and set it up only.

Know a stroke in seconds: BE FAST

At the first sign, pull over, note the time, don't drive yourself, and call 911. Stroke care works best within about three hours of the first symptom.

B — BalanceSudden loss of balance or dizziness
E — EyesSudden trouble seeing in one or both eyes
F — FaceOne side of the face droops when smiling
A — ArmsOne arm drifts down when both are raised
S — SpeechSpeech is slurred or hard to understand
T — TimeCall 911 now; note when it started
Information only — not medical advice. BE FAST is a public awareness guide, not a diagnosis. When in doubt, call emergency services.
