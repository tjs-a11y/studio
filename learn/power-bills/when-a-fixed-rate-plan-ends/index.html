What happens when your fixed-rate electricity plan ends

When a fixed-rate term ends, the next step depends on the plan terms and the rules in your market. Some plans move to a month-to-month variable rate that can change; do not assume the new price will be higher or that every provider follows the same process. Here is what to check before the date arrives.

The short answer

A fixed-rate plan locks a price per kilowatt-hour for a stated term. When the term ends, the next step depends on your contract and market rules. Some retail plans move to a month-to-month variable plan, where the price can change. Check the renewal notice and the plan documents rather than assuming every provider follows the same process.

What the rules say

The Public Utility Commission of Texas consumer page explains that a variable-rate plan has no fixed term and that its price per kilowatt-hour can vary from month to month. That is Texas consumer guidance, not a rule for every state or every utility. Read your own contract and renewal notice to see whether the plan changes automatically, what rate applies next and whether any fee or action deadline applies.

Why the next rate can change

A variable rate can move up or down from month to month. A renewal offer may also differ from a new-customer offer, but the direction and amount depend on the provider, market and contract. Compare the actual next rate and all recurring charges rather than assuming a plan end date automatically means a price increase.

How to get ahead of it

The fix is to read the notice early. Find your plan's end date in the contract or electricity facts label, check how and when the provider will notify you, and compare available plans before the deadline. Review any cancellation fee, renewal term, variable-rate formula and action required. If you are already on a month-to-month plan, check its actual cancellation terms before switching.
