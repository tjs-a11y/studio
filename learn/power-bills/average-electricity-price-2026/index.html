The average U.S. residential electricity price in August 2026

EIA’s latest Electric Power Monthly reports an August 2026 U.S. residential average of 18.16 cents per kilowatt-hour, compared with 17.57 cents in August 2025. The 2026 value is preliminary, and your own plan rate may differ substantially.

Source checked October 31, 2026 · EIA release date October 23, 2026

Latest published national figure

The U.S. Energy Information Administration’s Electric Power Monthly, released October 23, 2026, reports an average residential electricity price of 18.16 cents per kilowatt-hour for August 2026. The comparable August 2025 figure was 17.57 cents, a year-over-year increase of about 3.4%. EIA labels 2026 values as preliminary estimates based on a cutoff-model sample; 2025 values are final. The table also reports state and census-division averages, which vary widely.

What the average does and does not mean

This is a national sector average, not a live offer from an electricity supplier and not a prediction for a particular household. Your actual bill depends on location, utility, provider, customer class, usage, delivery charges, fixed fees, taxes, credits and the terms of your plan. A national benchmark is useful context, not a price target or proof that an individual bill is too high.

How to compare your own bill

Find the billing-period kilowatt-hours and the charges that apply to that usage. Separate the energy or supply rate from delivery charges, fixed monthly fees, taxes, credits and one-time adjustments. Dividing the total bill by kWh gives an all-in average for that month; it is not necessarily the supply rate in your contract. For a plan comparison, read the plan document or electricity facts label and compare offers at the same usage level.

Why the fixed-plan end date matters

A fixed-rate plan may end or renew under terms that differ by provider and jurisdiction. In Texas, the Public Utility Commission explains fixed and variable plans and tells consumers to review the plan details. Do not assume every state or provider uses the same default plan. Check your contract for the end date, renewal notice and rate that applies if you take no action.
