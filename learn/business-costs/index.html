Recurring business costs: auto-renewals, price increases and forgotten subscriptions

Software, services and supplies that renew on their own are easy to forget. These pages show how to find the deadline in a contract, catch price creep before it renews, and pull every recurring charge out of a bank or accounting export.

Why these costs slip through

Most small businesses pay for a long list of things that renew without anyone pressing a button: software subscriptions, phone and internet plans, equipment leases, insurance, maintenance contracts, payroll and bookkeeping services, listing and marketing fees. Each one was a reasonable decision on the day it was signed. The trouble comes later. The person who signed it leaves or forgets. The contract renews for another year because the notice window closed a month before anyone looked. The price goes up a little at each renewal, a few seats get added and never removed, and the same tool ends up paid for twice by two departments.

None of this needs special software to fix. It needs three habits, and each page below covers one: knowing every cancel-by date before it passes, checking the price at each renewal and asking for a better one, and reviewing the transactions themselves a few times a year.

The pages are general guidance. Contracts differ, and the wording of yours is what counts. Where a federal rule is relevant we link the official text and say exactly what it covers; where we could not verify something, we say so.
