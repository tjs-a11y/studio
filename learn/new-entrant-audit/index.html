The 16 violations that automatically fail an FMCSA new entrant safety audit

A new carrier fails its FMCSA safety audit automatically if the auditor finds any one of 16 specific violations. Each page below explains one of them in plain English, with the regulation text, the records involved and how to fix it.

How the new entrant program works

A new US motor carrier is a "new entrant" and is monitored for 18 months. During that period FMCSA conducts a safety audit once the carrier has been operating long enough to have records to review, which the rule says will generally be at least 3 months, and the carrier must make all required records available to the certified auditor on request (49 CFR 385.307).

A carrier can fail in two ways: a general lack of basic safety management controls (judged under Appendix A to Part 385), or a violation of any one of 16 regulations listed in 49 CFR 385.321(b). Those 16 fail the audit automatically. Fourteen fail on a single occurrence; two (hours-of-service records and periodic inspections) fail only when 51% or more of the examined records are in violation (49 CFR 385.321).

What happens after a failed audit

FMCSA sends written notice no later than 45 days after the audit. Most carriers then have 60 days from the date of the notice to take the corrective actions it lists; passenger carriers (9 or more passengers including the driver, for compensation, or more than 15) and certain hazmat carriers have 45 days (49 CFR 385.319). FMCSA may extend the 60-day period by up to 60 more days for a carrier making a good-faith effort, and the 45-day period by up to 10 days in limited cases (49 CFR 385.323). Without an accepted response, registration is revoked and an out-of-service order takes effect on day 61 or day 46 from the notice date, and the carrier may not operate in interstate commerce (49 CFR 385.325).
