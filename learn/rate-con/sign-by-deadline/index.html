Sign-by deadline

Some rate cons say they must be signed and returned within a few minutes or the load goes to someone else. Sometimes that's real competition for freight; sometimes it's pressure designed to stop you reading.

Why short deadlines exist

Spot freight moves fast. A broker with a hot load may really need an answer quickly, and a carrier who hesitates loses it. But the deadline is on the paperwork, not just the phone call: the rate con itself says "must be signed within 30 minutes". That gives you half an hour to read terms that may include fines, setoff and claims deductions.

Urgency is also a common feature of fraud: a party posing as a broker wants the load moving before anyone checks its MC number. A tight deadline isn't proof of anything, but it's a reason to make your checks fast rather than skip them.

A five-minute reading routine

  1. Total rate: does it match what you agreed? (One minute.)
  2. Broker identity: does the MC/DOT match the company name on FMCSA's public record? Is the email on the company's own domain? See broker verification.
  3. Money terms: detention, TONU, lumper, payment terms.
  4. Fine print: search the text for "fine", "deduct", "offset", "claim", "adjust", "arbitration".
  5. Stops and times: appointment or FCFS, and reefer temperature if it applies.

What the Rate Con Checker flags

The checker looks for phrases like "must be signed", "sign and return", "signed within" or "return signed", followed by a number of minutes or hours. If it finds one, it's a flag: "Must be signed within N minutes. Read it all first." It doesn't suggest a fix-request line, because the deadline isn't a term you negotiate; it's a reason to read faster.

If you need more time

Say so: "I'll sign as soon as I've read it; give me ten minutes." A legitimate broker who wants your truck will usually wait. If the answer is "sign now or lose it", you've learned something about how disputes on this load would go.

Signing doesn't end the reading

If you did sign quickly, read the rest of the rate con before the truck rolls. If something unacceptable turns up, raise it in writing right away; a change agreed before pickup is far easier than a dispute after delivery. Save the signed copy with the load file either way.
