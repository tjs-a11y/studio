Fines, deductions and chargebacks

Many rate cons list fines that are taken out of your pay: late delivery, missed check calls, tracking gaps, missing paperwork, late invoices. Each one may look small; together they can wipe out a load's profit.

Where fines hide

Fines are usually in the fine print below the rates, or in a "carrier requirements" block that's the same on every load. Common examples:

  • Late pickup or delivery, per hour or as a flat amount.
  • Missing or late bill of lading (BOL) or proof of delivery (POD).
  • Tracking not active or turned off; see tracking requirements.
  • Missed check calls.
  • Re-consignment, unapproved stops, or "service failure".
  • Chargebacks passed through from the shipper or receiver.

Add them up before you sign

Read the fines as if every one applied. In the checker's sample rate con, a $250 tracking fine, $150 per hour late and a $100 paperwork deduction appear in three different paragraphs. One bad day at a receiver could cost more than the load's margin. Knowing the total turns a vague worry into a number you can negotiate.

Fines versus actual losses

A fine is a fixed amount set in advance. An actual loss is a cost someone can show, such as a receiver's documented charge for a missed appointment. Many carriers accept being responsible for documented losses they caused while pushing back on fixed penalties. Asking "what loss does this fine cover?" is a fair question.

What the Rate Con Checker flags

The checker looks for "fine", "penalty", "deduct", "chargeback" or "withhold" within a short distance of a dollar amount, on either side. It lists how many fines it found, the largest, and the total if all applied.

  • Largest fine at or above your maximum (default $500; Pro users set their own): red flag.
  • Fines found below that: flag.
  • Penalty language with no dollar amount: flag, because you can't price it.
  • None found: OK.

Text extraction isn't perfect. A fine written as "two hundred fifty dollars", or split across lines in a PDF, can slip past. Always read the fine print yourself.

What to ask for

Please remove or cap the fines listed above. I can't agree to deductions that aren't tied to an actual documented loss.

If a fine stays, ask for the condition to be specific. "Late" should mean after the appointment time plus a grace period, measured by the facility's check-in record, not "late in broker's judgment". And check whether fines can be taken from other loads; if so, that's a setoff problem too.
