Rate confirmation glossary: the terms that cost truckers money

A rate confirmation is short, but a few lines in it decide whether a load makes money. This glossary explains each term in plain English, what the wording usually means, and what to ask the broker to change before you sign.

Each entry covers one term carriers run into on broker rate cons: what it means, the wording to look for, what the Rate Con Checker flags, and a sentence you can send the broker. Where a federal rule is relevant (hours of service, broker records, the broker bond, the cargo claims process), the entry quotes it from the Code of Federal Regulations and links to it. Where something is a business practice rather than a rule, such as factoring or quick pay, the entry says so.

The Rate Con Checker runs 20 rules over a pasted rate con, in your browser, and grades it. Every "what the checker flags" note in this glossary describes those rules exactly, including their default thresholds (for example: detention under $50 an hour, more than 2 free hours, payment terms over 30 days, a single fine of $500 or more). Those defaults are settings you can change in Pro, not industry standards.

Start with what a rate con is if you're new to brokered freight. Otherwise jump to the term you're looking at.
