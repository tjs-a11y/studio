How to challenge inaccurate FMCSA safety data through DataQs

Submit a Request for Data Review (RDR) in FMCSA’s DataQs system with a specific factual or legal reason and supporting records; the agency responsible for the data reviews it.

What changed in 2026

FMCSA announced revised DataQs requirements on April 15, 2026. On October 16, 2026, the agency announced that all 50 states, Washington, D.C. and U.S. territories had implemented the updated framework. The changes give carriers a more defined process for asking an agency to review safety information they believe is incomplete, incorrect or assigned to the wrong record.

The three-stage timelines described here apply to state and territorial agencies receiving FMCSA Motor Carrier Safety Assistance Program (MCSAP) funding. DataQs can route requests about federal and state information, but the agency responsible for the record handles the review. Check the agency shown in your request and the applicable state implementation plan rather than assuming every request follows the same clock.

Before you sign in: DataQs requires multifactor authentication

The current DataQs login requires multifactor authentication (MFA). If you previously used a DataQs username and password, the login page directs you to sign in through Login.gov with the same email address as your DataQs account. Motor carriers and FMCSA or State partners who have not accessed DataQs before must first use their FMCSA Portal account; drivers and public/industry users can register for a DataQs account. Follow the role-specific instructions shown on the official login page.

State DataQs review stages and timelines

  • Initial review: the state must open an initial RDR within seven days of submission and issue its decision within 21 days. When denying a correction, the decision must not be made solely by the issuing officer.
  • Reconsideration: if you ask for reconsideration, an independent subject-matter reviewer who was not involved in the initial decision reviews it. The decision is due within 21 days after escalation.
  • Final review: a senior decision-maker or independent panel completes the final stage within 45 days after escalation.
  • Appeal window: submit a reconsideration or final-review request within 30 days of the decision at the previous stage.
  • More information: if the state asks you for additional information, respond within 14 days. The state’s decision clock pauses while it waits for that response.

For state RDRs, FMCSA’s published eligibility windows are three years from an inspection and five years from a crash. These are request windows, not a promise that a request will be accepted or that the underlying record will change.

Records to gather before you file

Identify the exact record and the exact field you believe is wrong. A general statement that an inspection was unfair is not enough. The requestor carries the burden of providing a factual or legal reason and supporting documentation.

  • The original inspection report or crash report, including its report number, date, state and the carrier, driver or vehicle identifiers.
  • Any citation, court disposition, adjudication record or official document that changes the status or outcome of the event.
  • Supporting records tied to the disputed fact: ELD or log records, dispatch records, bills of lading, maintenance records, photographs, repair invoices or other contemporaneous documents, as relevant.
  • The specific item you want reviewed, the correction you believe is accurate, and a concise explanation of how each attachment supports that correction.
  • Your DataQs request ID, copies of prior decisions, dates of those decisions and proof of when you submitted each stage.

How to make the request easier to review

  1. Confirm that the report belongs to the correct carrier, driver, vehicle and event. Use the state where the event occurred when the form asks for the event state.
  2. Quote the report number and identify the precise entry you dispute. Explain what is inaccurate, incomplete or misattributed and what the record should say instead.
  3. Attach documents that directly support the correction. Label each file and connect it to a specific statement in your explanation.
  4. Submit through the official DataQs system and save the request ID, submission date and copies of every attachment.
  5. Read the written decision. If the state denies the correction, use the next available review stage only when you have a specific factual or legal basis, and calendar the 30-day request window.

What DataQs does not guarantee

A DataQs request is a request to review data, not an automatic deletion process. A valid violation does not become inaccurate because it affects a BASIC or percentile. A submission, a missed target date or a new review stage does not by itself prove that an officer, carrier, broker or agency acted improperly. The agency may leave a record unchanged when the evidence does not support a correction, and the result does not guarantee a change to a safety rating, percentile or insurance decision.

Programs and records with different procedures

The DataQs Help Center says the Crash Preventability Determination Program and Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse petitions have program-specific procedures and timelines. Do not apply the state MCSAP RDR deadlines above to those processes. If the record is federal rather than state-reported, follow the instructions and response path for the agency responsible for that record. State MCSAP implementation plans are available through the official DataQs system.

This guide explains the published process as checked on November 1, 2026. It is not a legal opinion about a particular inspection, crash, citation or carrier record.
