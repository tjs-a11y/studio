          How it works

          What changed for 2026?

          For payments made after December 31, 2025, the federal line for Form 1099-NEC (services) and Form 1099-MISC (rent and similar payments) is $2,000 paid to one payee in the year. It used to be $600. The change is in 26 U.S.C. §6041(a) and §6041A(a), made by Pub. L. 119-21 §70433. From 2027 the amount is indexed for inflation (§6041(h)); the 2027 figure isn't published yet. Royalties still count from $10 (§6050N).

          The IRS regulations still show the old $600 figure because they haven't been rewritten yet; the statute controls. Your state may have its own rule.

          What can't a bank file tell us?

          Whether a payee is a corporation, whether you paid for services or goods, and who a check went to (most bank exports show only the check number). That's why we ask, and why every result says "likely". Card payments are left out because card processors report them on Form 1099-K. Payment-app payments are counted but flagged, because it depends on how they were sent.

          How are renewals and price increases found?

          We group charges by vendor and look for a regular rhythm: weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly, with amounts that stay within 15% or step up. An increase of 5% or more is marked amber, 15% or more red. The next renewal is the last charge plus the rhythm, so it's a guess: check the vendor's terms.
