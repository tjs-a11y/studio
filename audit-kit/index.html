    The new entrant audit, in plain English

    What is it, and when does it happen?

    New carriers are "new entrants" and are monitored for 18 months (49 CFR 385.307). The safety audit comes once you have enough records to review, generally after at least 3 months of operation (385.307(b)). FMCSA generally aims to finish it within your first 12 months (sooner for passenger carriers); that target is not in the regulation text, so exact timing varies. Some events, like a roadside out-of-service order or a positive drug test, can trigger an expedited audit (385.308). Get ready from day one.

    What happens if I fail?

    FMCSA sends a written notice within 45 days of the audit. You then have 60 days from the notice date to fix the problems and send proof (45 days for passenger carriers and certain hazmat carriers). If you don't, your new entrant registration is revoked and an out-of-service order takes effect on day 61 (or day 46), and you must stop interstate operations (49 CFR 385.319(c), 385.325). Read the notice the day it arrives.

    Why do some rules say "51% of records"?

    For hours-of-service records (395.8(a)) and periodic inspections (396.17(a)), the 385.321(b) table says a fail needs a pattern: violations in 51% or more of the records examined. Every other rule on the list fails on a single occurrence. Don't aim for 50%. Every record should be right.

    My trucks don't need a CDL. What applies?

    Drug and alcohol testing (Part 382) and the CDL rules (Part 383) apply to drivers of vehicles that need a CDL: generally 26,001 lbs or more, 16 or more passengers, or placarded hazmat (see 49 CFR 383.5). Medical cards, hours of service, inspections and insurance still apply to most interstate carriers with vehicles of 10,001 lbs or more. Check the definitions before you skip anything.
